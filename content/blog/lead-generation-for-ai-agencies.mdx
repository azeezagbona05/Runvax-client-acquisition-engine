Lead generation for an AI agency comes down to five decisions: pick one vertical, define the operational pain you solve in one sentence, build a list of businesses that plausibly have that pain, run outbound across email and WhatsApp with a 4 to 5 touch follow-up over about three weeks, and track every prospect through a pipeline so nothing goes cold. Agencies that skip the vertical decision and pitch "AI automation" to everyone get the reply rates, under 4%, that signal broken targeting.

This is part of the [AI for client acquisition pillar](/blog/ai-for-client-acquisition-guide) and follows [how to find businesses that need AI automation](/blog/finding-businesses-that-need-ai-automation). The general agency version is [lead generation for agencies](/blog/lead-generation-for-agencies); this one is specific to selling AI.

## Decision 1: One Vertical

An AI agency that serves "any business" has no referral network, no reusable demo, and no credibility shortcut. One vertical gives you all three. Fastest-ROI options in 2026: e-commerce operations, B2B SaaS sales ops, HR and recruitment, and customer support. For local services, dental, legal, real estate, and hospitality are less competitive to reach and have visible buying signals.

Three tests help you choose between candidates:

1. **Enquiry-driven or appointment-driven.** The businesses that benefit most from an AI enquiry system are the ones where a customer message turns into a booking or a sale: clinics, dentists, salons, gyms, law firms, estate agents, hotels, schools. A business that sells from a shelf has less to automate.
2. **High value per customer.** One recovered patient, tenant or client has to be worth more than your monthly fee, or the retainer is hard to justify. High-ticket categories make the maths easy.
3. **A person you can name.** The buyer should be one identifiable role: the practice owner, the front desk manager, the principal. If you cannot say who signs off, you cannot write the message.

## Decision 2: The One-Sentence Pain

Write the sentence a prospect would nod at: "You lose enquiries that come in after 6pm." "Your team re-keys every order between the store and the fulfilment system." If you cannot write it, you do not know your offer well enough to sell it cold.

A good pain sentence has an event, a consequence and a frequency. A weak one names a technology. Compare:

- Weak: "We build AI chatbots for dental practices."
- Strong: "When someone messages your practice after the front desk closes, they usually book with whoever answers first."

The strong version is also phrased as a condition, not a claim. That matters, and it comes up again in decision 3.

Whatever source you use, a good list does two things in a specific order, and the order is the part most agencies get wrong.

**First, it checks that the business is real and healthy.** A dormant business can look like a perfect target: no website, no booking, nobody answering. It is also a worthless lead, because there is nobody there to buy. Runvax scores business strength out of 100 before it looks for any opportunity, using review count (a business with 500 or more reviews earns the most), rating, whether the listing is still operational, whether there has been a review in the last 90 days, and whether a phone number, opening hours and a website are listed. Anything below 45 is dropped, and the shortlist is capped at 25. The list is a cut, not a sort: a business that does not qualify is absent, not ranked last. You can run the same check by hand: reviews in the last quarter, a rating above 4, a working phone number.

**Second, it looks for something specific to sell.** For an AI system, a business only qualifies if a customer has publicly mentioned a problem reaching or booking with them, or if it has 50 or more reviews in a trade where enquiries naturally pile up, such as appointment-based or enquiry-heavy categories.

### Why Most of Your List Is Inference

Customer complaint language, someone writing "nobody picks up" or "had to chase them for days," appears in only around 2% of businesses on real data. So for nearly every prospect, your case rests on what is true of the trade rather than evidence about that business. - Dental sits near the top of the category range, with a ceiling of 92.
- Its own signals (high review volume, strong rating, already has a website) lift it to about 86 out of 100.
- With no customer-reported friction its confidence is low, so it is shown as "good" rather than "strong".

## Decision 4: The Outbound Motion

- **Channels:** email plus WhatsApp where locally common, plus a call for warm-but-quiet leads. Multi-channel produces around 287% more leads than single-channel.
- **Cadence:** 4 to 5 touches over about 21 days. Vary the angle each time; do not just "check in". See [how many follow-ups before giving up](/blog/how-many-follow-ups-before-giving-up).
- **Opener:** name the pain and the moment, not the technology.
- **One angle per message.** Pick missed enquiries, staff workload, speed of response or customer experience, and argue from only that one. Two lenses in one message reads as a pitch deck.
- **Regulated trades need a guardrail.** For clinics and similar trades, never imply the system gives clinical or medical advice. It handles enquiries and bookings only. Say so in the message.
- **Volume target:** enough that your reply rate (aim for 8% or more) produces the number of discovery calls your close rate needs.

A sensible five-touch shape: touch one names the after-hours scenario; touch two offers a short demo built from their public information; touch three shares one concrete example from a similar business in the same trade; touch four is a WhatsApp or call; touch five is a polite close that leaves the door open. To size volume, work backwards from the 8% reply target: at that rate, 40 contacted prospects produce about three replies, so plan a list several times larger than the number of conversations you want, then adjust from your real numbers.

## Decision 5: The Pipeline

Track every prospect through stages (found, contacted, replied, call booked, proposal, won or lost) with a follow-up flag on anything untouched for three days. Most AI agency revenue is lost not on the pitch but in the gap between a warm reply and the next message. Modern pipelines run the full lifecycle, not just a contact list.

Review it weekly with three questions: how many new qualified prospects entered, how many moved a stage, and which stage is leaking. If replies are fine but calls are not booking, the offer is clear but the next step is vague. If contacts are not turning into replies, the list or the opener is wrong, and more volume will not fix it.

## Common Failure Modes

- **Pitching the technology.** Prospects buy fewer missed enquiries, not "an LLM-powered assistant".
- **Claiming a failure you did not see.** Telling a clinic it is losing patients, with no evidence, ends the thread.
- **Chasing dormant businesses.** No site and no activity is a closed shop, not an opportunity.
- **Mixing verticals in one week.** You lose the reusable demo, which is the main advantage of a single vertical.
- **Stopping at touch two.** Most replies come after the first follow-up.
