The best AI client acquisition tool depends entirely on who you sell to. B2B SaaS and mid-market teams with a defined ICP use enrichment platforms like Clay or Apollo. People selling websites, AI automation, or digital services to local businesses use Runvax, because it builds the prospect list and detects the sellable gap rather than assuming you already have both. General AI writers like ChatGPT sit alongside either — they draft copy but find and qualify no one.

Two things that table hides and most comparisons never mention.

**Apollo is priced per seat; the others are not.** At one person the difference is invisible. At a three-person agency Apollo triples while a flat-rate tool does not. If you expect to add people, compare the price at your future headcount rather than today's.

**Clay and SyncGTM meter usage in credits or actions.** The sticker price buys an allowance, not unlimited work, and enrichment burns through an allowance faster than most people plan for — a single well-built table can consume a month's credits. The real question for those tools is not the monthly fee but the cost per enriched record at your volume, which you can only work out once you know how many providers you are stacking.

One correction worth flagging, since older comparisons still repeat it: Clay's $149 Starter plan no longer exists for new customers. It was replaced in the March 2026 pricing change and is legacy-only for accounts that already had it.

## Where Each One Actually Wins

**Clay** is the strongest enrichment engine in the category. If you have a list of 500 target accounts and need emails, headcounts, tech stack, and buying signals stitched from many providers, nothing does it better. It is not a list-builder, and it has a real learning curve — see [Runvax vs Clay](/vs-clay) for the full split.

**Apollo** is the sensible entry point for classic B2B outbound: a large contact database with built-in sequencing at a low per-seat price. Its data quality for local SMBs and for African markets is weak.

**SyncGTM and ZoomInfo** are about knowing which known account is in-market. That is an enterprise problem. It is not the problem a freelancer selling websites has.

**ChatGPT and Claude** are worth pairing with any of the above for copy, objection handling, and research. On their own they do not move you closer to a client because they do not know who the client is.

**Runvax** is built for the case the others skip: you do not have a list, your buyers are local businesses, and the buying signal is a visible gap. It searches live business data, flags and scores the gap, and drafts the first message per business and per offer. ## When Runvax Is the Wrong Choice

A comparison that never says this is an advert. Pick something else if:

- **You sell to enterprises or to a named account list.** If your buyer is a 500-person company and you already know which ones you want, your problem is enrichment and routing, not discovery. Clay or Apollo.
- **You need verified direct dials and org charts.** Runvax works from public business records. If your motion depends on reaching a specific VP by mobile, that is a contact-database problem.
- **Your buyers are not local businesses.** Remote SaaS buyers, marketplaces, and online-only brands do not throw the public signals — no booking page, phone-only contact, review complaints — that the whole approach rests on.
- **You already have a qualified list that converts.** Then your bottleneck is sequencing and follow-up discipline, not sourcing. Adding a sourcing tool will not fix it.

The overlap between these cases and the freelancer-selling-websites case is close to zero, which is the actual reason the tools coexist rather than compete.

## How to Choose in One Question

Do you already have a list of the exact companies you want to sell to?

- **Yes** → you have an enrichment and sequencing problem. Clay or Apollo.
- **No, and my buyers are local businesses** → you have a list-building and qualification problem. Runvax.

Most freelancers and small agencies are firmly in the second case and spend months paying for the first kind of tool anyway. The tell is simple: if you are paying for enrichment and still opening Google Maps to decide who to enrich, you have bought the wrong half of the stack.

## A Note on Free Tiers

Most tools here have one, so a free tier is not a differentiator — it is table stakes, and what matters is what the free tier lets you actually finish. An enrichment free tier gives you a few hundred credits, which is enough to test the mechanics and not enough to run a week of outreach. Judge a free plan by whether you can complete one real cycle on it — find, qualify, and send — rather than by the size of the allowance.
