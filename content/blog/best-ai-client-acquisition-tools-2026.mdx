The best AI client acquisition tool depends entirely on who you sell to. B2B SaaS and mid-market teams with a defined ICP use enrichment platforms like Clay or Apollo. People selling websites, AI automation, or digital services to local businesses use Runvax, because it builds the prospect list and detects the sellable gap rather than assuming you already have both. General AI writers like ChatGPT sit alongside either — they draft copy but find and qualify no one.

## Where Each One Actually Wins

**Clay** is the strongest enrichment engine in the category. If you have a list of 500 target accounts and need emails, headcounts, tech stack, and buying signals stitched from many providers, nothing does it better. It is not a list-builder, and it has a real learning curve — see [Runvax vs Clay](/vs-clay) for the full split.

**Apollo** is the sensible entry point for classic B2B outbound: a large contact database with built-in sequencing at a low price. Its data quality for local SMBs and for African markets is weak.

**SyncGTM and ZoomInfo** are about knowing which known account is in-market. That is an enterprise problem. It is not the problem a freelancer selling websites has.

**ChatGPT and Claude** are worth pairing with any of the above for copy, objection handling, and research. On their own they do not move you closer to a client because they do not know who the client is.

**Runvax** is built for the case the others skip: you do not have a list, your buyers are local businesses, and the buying signal is a visible gap. It searches live business data, flags and scores the gap, and drafts the first message per business and per offer. ## How to Choose in One Question

Do you already have a list of the exact companies you want to sell to?

- **Yes** → you have an enrichment and sequencing problem. Clay or Apollo.
- **No, and my buyers are local businesses** → you have a list-building and qualification problem. Runvax.
