Cold outreach for an AI automation agency works when the first message opens on a specific moment from the prospect's week — an enquiry that went unanswered, a task done by hand — and never mentions the technology. Keep it under 80 words on WhatsApp and under 150 in email, close on one low-friction question, and follow up 4–5 times over about 21 days. The word "AI" belongs in message two, once, after they have engaged.

## Why "AI" Kills the First Message

Lead with the technology and a cautious owner immediately thinks: cost, disruption, "we're not ready". Lead with the outcome in their own words and they think about the problem you just named. Only one of those gets a reply.

## The First-Message Structure

Three beats, nothing more:

1. **Greeting + one genuine nod.** "Saw you've got 200+ reviews at 4.8 — that's a real reputation."
2. **The moment + the possibility.** "Someone messages you at 9pm about an appointment. Right now it waits till morning. It could be answered and booked before you're back."
3. **One question.** "Worth a quick look at how that would work for [business]?"

### WhatsApp script (72 words)

> Good afternoon Sarah! Had a look at the practice — 300+ reviews at 4.7 says a lot about how you look after patients. One thing I noticed: enquiries that land in the evening or on weekends sit until someone's back at the desk. That gap can be closed, so a patient messaging at 9pm gets an answer and a booked slot the same night. Worth a quick look at how that would work for Bright Dental?

### Email script (128 words)

> Subject: enquiries landing after hours at Bright Dental
>
> Hi Sarah,
>
> You've built something with Bright Dental — 300 reviews at 4.7 is not easy.
>
> One thing that stood out: your site holds all the information a patient needs, but there's no way for someone messaging in the evening or at the weekend to actually get an answer or book. Those enquiries wait, and some of them call another practice first.
>
> That part can be handled automatically — a patient asks a question at 9pm, gets a real answer, and the appointment is booked, all before you're back in.
>
> If it's useful, I can put together how that would look specifically for Bright Dental. Would that be worth 15 minutes?
>
> [name]

## The Follow-Up Cadence

| Touch | Day | Angle |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0 | The moment + possibility (above) |
| 2 | 3 | A concrete example: walk one enquiry end to end |
| 3 | 7 | New angle — the reputation cost of slow replies, with a review quote |
| 4 | 14 | Offer to map it to their exact setup, no commitment |
| 5 | 21 | Short "breakup" — close the loop, leave the door open |

Most replies come from touches 2–4. Personalising the opener lifts reply rates about 30.5%; 2026 cold outbound averages 6–9%, so aim for 8%+ and treat under 4% as a targeting problem.

## Message Two: Now You Can Say It

They replied. Answer what they asked, then explain the opportunity concretely — one enquiry, end to end, in their trade's language. You may say "AI" once, never as the subject. Do not send the build plan or the price yet; that is message three.

## The Reply Ladder

The most common way this outreach fails is not silence — it is answering message two with message five. A prospect asks "how much?" on their first reply, the agency sends a full scope and a price, and the conversation ends there, because a number with no established problem behind it is only ever too big.

Each rung does one job and hands over to the next:

| Rung | Their message | Your job — and only this |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Any reply at all | Answer the literal question. Confirm the specific gap you named applies. |
| 2 | "How would that work?" | One enquiry walked end to end, in their words. Offer to show it live. |
| 3 | "What does it cost?" | Range plus what moves it. Push for the call: the number depends on scope you have not seen. |
| 4 | On the call | Discovery, then the demo built on their own public information. |
| 5 | After the call | Scope and price, within 24 hours, one page. |

The discipline is refusing to skip rungs even when the prospect pulls you up one. "How much?" at rung 1 is not a request for a quote — it is a test of whether you understand their situation. "It depends on whether you want just the after-hours enquiries or the whole booking flow — can I ask what happens to those at the moment?" answers honestly and returns to rung 2.

## Sending It Without Burning Your Domain

None of the copy above matters if the messages do not arrive.

- **Never send cold email from your main domain.** Use a separate sending domain so a deliverability problem cannot take down the address your existing clients use.
- **Warm up before volume.** A new domain sending fifty cold emails on day one gets filtered. Build up over a few weeks.
- **Keep daily volume boring.** A small agency does not need hundreds a day; thirty well-targeted messages beats three hundred generic ones, and it is also what keeps you out of spam folders.
- **Authenticate properly** — SPF, DKIM and DMARC on the sending domain. This is a one-time setup and the single most common reason a technically fine campaign lands nowhere.
- **WhatsApp is not exempt.** It is more forgiving on filters and far less forgiving socially: one careless bulk blast to a local trade community and your number is known. Same targeting discipline, lower volume.

## What Not to Do

The failure patterns in this niche are specific and repetitive:

- **Listing capabilities.** "We build AI agents, chatbots, workflow automations and custom integrations" tells an owner nothing about their business and reads as a brochure.
- **Attaching a deck to message one.** Nobody opens it, and it signals this is a broadcast.
- **Fake personalisation.** "I loved your website!" is worse than no personalisation, because it proves you are running a template. Name something only someone who looked would know.
- **Pitching the build before the problem is agreed.** If they have not conceded the gap exists, every feature you describe is solving nothing.
- **Following up with "just bumping this."** A follow-up with no new information trains them to ignore the thread. Every touch in the cadence above carries a new angle for exactly this reason.
